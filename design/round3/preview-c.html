BeatSlopper · Direction C · Clearwave

What it optimises for: reading and reaching. One view owns the whole width, so a 16-step grid, a 3-octave roll and a 16-insert mixer each get the room they never had; everything else is one click away on a left rail, with the TRACKS list sliding in beside it and the keyboard docked at the bottom, collapsible. Surfaces are flat white cards with 1 px borders (no paired shadows, so text contrast is the paper's, not the shadow's), type is 14 / 12 with condensed tabular numerals for values, every hit target is 32 pt, every scrollable area shows a 10 pt scrollbar, and one teal accent does the talking next to the eight track colours. It is light first because a beat-maker at 150 % on a 4K panel is mostly read in daylight; the dark theme is a true inversion with the same contrast ratios, not a dim copy. What it gives up: the "instrument" feeling of the neumorphic console (nothing glows, nothing looks pressed); seeing synth and sequencer at once (the synth is a view, with the preset browser as a slide-over); and some density: Ableton-grade whitespace means the SYNTH view scrolls at 800 pt tall.
